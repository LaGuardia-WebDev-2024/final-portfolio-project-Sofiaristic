About Me

New York City is the place that bristles with cultural and social diversity. To paraphrase Frank Sinatra, if you've lived here, it’s like you've lived everywhere, experiencing countless lives within one place. Growing up amid these contrasts, I became attuned to the contrasts in how people live, work, and act. My father, a blue-collar worker, and my mother, a healthcare professional, showed me resilience and care from different perspectives. At school, I sat alongside both middle-class kids and those from wealthy and influential families. On my commutes, I've shared subway cars with strangers from every background. Across all these experiences, I saw how so much of our world often goes unnoticed and undervalued. We overlook the efforts of ordinary people, we neglect the environment, and we underestimate our ability to change. Through my art, I seek to redirect attention to what is unseen, undervalued and ignored. My work advocates for a better world through social, environmental and cultural themes combined with the idea of hope for a better future. I don't paint problems just to expose them, I paint to reveal possibility, worth and connection amongst us all.

Art, to me, is one of the strongest voices humanity has. It transcends language, culture, and class binding us together in a way words cannot. I create because I want to be part of that collective voice, to remind us of the beauty in what we take for granted, and to spark the change that begins with simply looking closely.
